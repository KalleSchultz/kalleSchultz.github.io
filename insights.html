Leadership & AI

When AI does the work for you — who leads?

Notes from a recent leadership workshop I ran with Anna Philipson for the Good Accelerator network.

We recently held a session with a room full of senior leaders. Not a seminar. Not a lecture. A conversation — where experience was given room to speak.

One thing struck me harder than expected. Most people in the room had already started using AI. Many were impressed. Some were worried. But almost everyone talked about AI as a tool for personal efficiency — faster emails, better material, shorter meetings. That's not wrong. It's just not enough.

The polished material isn't the problem. It's the asset.

During the workshop we used a case: you're handed a decision brief. It's well-structured, logical, easy to follow. It covers every angle. How do you know if it's actually good? How do you know if it's workable? How do you act on it?

The silence in the room said more than the answers did. Because the problem doesn't start when the material is bad — it starts when it looks so complete that no one dares question it. Your job as a leader isn't to distrust AI. It's to be the one who asks the questions AI can't ask itself.

Growth isn't built in personal efficiency. It's built in what happens between people.

This is where I think most organisations get stuck. The AI journey starts with the individual — and often stops there. But customer value is created along a journey. Growth lives in how teams collaborate, how decisions get made together, how an organisation moves fast as one — like a school of fish, not a group of faster individuals pulling in different directions.

If AI only raises individual pace without strengthening how the organisation moves together, we risk building teams that are locally efficient and globally slow.

Three risks we named, straight out of the room

  • Business power — Strategy stays on paper. Everything looks well thought through, but the organisation isn't moving with it.
  • Action power — Agility drops. Teams stop training themselves to move fast, because AI does it instead.
  • People power — Collective strength weakens. Trust and culture are built between people, in the room, not inside an algorithm.

What can you never hand to an algorithm?

That was our closing question to the group. The answers came fast: presence and relationships, direction and meaning, judgement in the unclear, moral courage. Those aren't soft values — they're the core of leadership, and what decides whether AI actually creates value in your organisation, or just impresses you on your laptop.

The question we're carrying into autumn isn't "are we using AI?" It's: how do you lead a school of fish when AI is in the water?

AI & ALT

Why sovereignty matters now

Who actually controls the data, the models, and the infrastructure your AI runs on.

One theme has only grown more urgent as AI has moved from pilot to production: sovereignty — who actually controls the data, the models, and the infrastructure your AI runs on. For Nordic and European organisations, that's no longer a compliance footnote. It's a board-level question about resilience, trust, and independence from any single vendor, cloud, or jurisdiction.

Having built one of the largest private clouds in Northern Europe, and later scaled AWS's hyperscale infrastructure, I've stood on both sides of that trade-off — and it shapes how we think about ALT. Sovereignty is built into ALT from day one, not bolted on afterwards: we give our customers real choice over where their data and models live — sovereign cloud, on-premise, or hyperscaler — so they can move at the speed AI demands without giving up control of what matters most.
